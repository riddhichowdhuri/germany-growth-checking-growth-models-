# Key Findings — Germany Growth & Development

## Solow

- Germany's growth is not fully explained by capital accumulation alone.
- The reported convergence relationship has the expected sign, but the relevant coefficients are statistically insignificant.
- The presentation reports weak empirical support for the basic Solow model.
- Structural and technological factors become more important in explaining Germany's modern growth.

## Ramsey–Cass–Koopmans

- Labour has a strong and statistically significant relationship with output in the reported Cobb–Douglas regression.
- The production-function regression reports R² ≈ 0.938.
- The lagged real interest rate coefficient in the reported GMM Euler equation is approximately 0.037 and statistically significant.
- The small coefficient is interpreted as a weak consumption response to interest-rate changes / low intertemporal substitution.
- The model adds a forward-looking behavioural channel through saving and consumption.

## OLG

- Demographic ageing and saving behaviour are central to the project's interpretation.
- Dynamic inefficiency is defined as r* < n + g.
- The presentation identifies periods satisfying this condition.
- Saving is described as increasing during periods of uncertainty, including 2008 and 2020.

## Overall

The project concludes that Germany's growth is better explained by combining Ramsey and OLG mechanisms with the Solow framework, because human capital, demographic transition, saving behaviour and institutions matter alongside capital accumulation.
